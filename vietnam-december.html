Vietnam in December: a different kind of winter

From misty mornings in Hanoi to sunlit southern shores, follow the changing rhythms of Vietnam at the end of the year.

By Wander editors   ·   November 16, 2026   ·   7 min read

Photo: Unsplash · Illustrative travel photography

In this story01 / The north02 / The central coast03 / The south04 / At the table05 / A thoughtful itinerary

December in Vietnam is not one single season. Travel from north to south and the mood changes with the landscape: cooler city mornings, a coast that asks for flexibility, and southern days that invite you to stay outside a little longer.

01 / The north

Hanoi mornings, mountain air

Start in Hanoi, where a cool morning can be best spent with a hot coffee and a wander through the Old Quarter. Notice the shopfronts, the morning routines, and the way a city gradually wakes around you.

Northern Vietnam can feel surprisingly cool in December, particularly in the mountains. Bring layers if you are heading toward Sa Pa, and allow room for fog or changing conditions to reshape your plans.

02 / The central coast

Lanterns, old streets, and a slower pace

Hoi An is a place to explore between appointments rather than through them. Walk its historic lanes, stop for a local meal, and let an afternoon unfold without trying to turn every corner into a photograph.

December can bring rain to parts of central Vietnam. Keep coastal plans flexible and check local forecasts before travelling. Museums, cafés, and cooking experiences are lovely ways to spend a wetter day.

“The best way to discover Vietnam is to leave a little of your day unplanned.”
03 / The south

A little more sunshine

Ho Chi Minh City offers a change of rhythm: markets, neighbourhood cafés, and an energy that rewards curious wandering. Try a morning walk before the day gets busy, then retreat somewhere shaded for lunch.

For beach time, consider adding Phu Quoc to a southern itinerary. Conditions vary, so choose accommodation and activities around your interests rather than expecting every day to look like a postcard.

04 / At the table

Follow the next good meal

Some of the most memorable travel moments arrive in a bowl. Look for a busy place with a short menu, try a regional speciality, and leave time for a second coffee. Ask before photographing the people making your food.

In Hanoi, a warm bowl of phở can anchor a cool morning. In Hoi An, seek out cao lầu. Farther south, explore the variety of fresh herbs, fruit, and dishes that make each stop feel distinct.

05 / A thoughtful itinerary

Leave room for the unexpected

For a shorter trip, choose one region and enjoy it properly. For a longer journey, connect two or three bases and allow for travel days. Vietnam is a country to experience, not a list to finish.

Pack a light rain layer, comfortable shoes, and warmer clothes for the north. Check current entry requirements, transport details, and local weather before booking. December travel is at its best when the plan has a little room to move.

A note for your journey
This is an inspirational editorial guide. Weather, access, and travel requirements can change. Check official local guidance and current conditions when planning your trip.
